# Why a real design system beats choosing a template

_First published at [bukkyai.duckdns.org/blog/why-good-design-system-beats-template](https://bukkyai.duckdns.org/blog/why-good-design-system-beats-template)._

Every website builder offers templates. Pick the "bakery" one, swap in your name, done. It's fast — and it's also the fastest way to look exactly like every other bakery that used the same template.

The alternative isn't slower, it's smarter: give each site its own **design system** — a small, consistent set of colors, fonts and spacing rules chosen for that business. It's what separates a site that feels like a brand from a site that feels like a page of options.

## What a design system actually is

It's not a thick brand book. It's roughly three decisions:

- **Two or three colors.** A background, a text color, and one accent for buttons and links.
- **Two fonts.** Usually a display font for headings and a clean font for body text.
- **One spacing rhythm.** The consistent padding that makes every section feel related.

That's it. Three decisions, repeated consistently, are worth more than a hundred random design choices.

## Why "chosen for you" reads better than "picked by you"

A template's colors fit the template. A design system's colors fit the business. A wellness brand and a rock venue might both get "dark" pages — but they shouldn't get the same dark page.

A palette chosen for a botanical skincare brand uses a muted green as the accent — `#4d6b45` — because that's the brand's "grown, not manufactured" story in color. Every button, link and heading uses it, so the whole site reinforces one idea.

## The test that matters: WCAG contrast

A beautiful palette is useless if the text can't be read. Real design systems check contrast between every text color and its background — meeting at least WCAG AA (4.5:1 for body text). That's not a bonus; it's part of the design decision. When contrast fails, the "designer" fixes the palette, not the excuse.

## How an AI builder can do this properly

Good AI website builders don't hand you a template to edit. From your one-paragraph brief they plan a design system *and* a theme — then write and design every page against that system. The colors you see in the preview are the colors your site ships with, and they're contrast-checked before you ever publish.

You can still override anything — swap a color, change a font, or pick a preset. But the default isn't "something generic." It's a system built for your business, from your words.

**The takeaway:** templates fit everyone and therefore no one. A three-decision design system, chosen for your business and checked for contrast, is what makes a site feel designed at all.
