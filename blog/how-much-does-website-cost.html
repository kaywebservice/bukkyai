How much does a small business website cost in 2026?

By the bukkyai team · 7 min read

It's the most-asked question in web design, and the standard answer — "it depends" — is technically true and completely useless. So let's give the real answer: a breakdown of what websites actually cost, what you get at each level, and where the money really goes.

The honest range

A small business website lands somewhere on this spectrum:

RouteUpfrontMonthlyWhat you get
DIY template builder$0–30$15–45A decent page, your own effort, locked to the platform
AI builder (bukkyai)$0$0–35A full written, designed site — you own the files
Freelancer$1,000–3,500$0–100A custom site, but you're the project manager
Design agency$5,000–15,000+$100–500Brand + site + ongoing work, on their timeline

The most expensive part isn't the design — it's the planning and the writing. Deciding what pages you need, and writing copy that sounds like you, is where human hours disappear. That's exactly the part most owners skip, and it's why so many expensive sites end up with filler like "Welcome to our website."

What's actually inside the price

  • The design — colors, fonts, layout. Small for a good system, huge for endless revisions.
  • The copy — every headline, section and page. Usually the biggest line item, and the one owners forget to budget for.
  • The build — turning the design into a working site.
  • Maintenance — the subscription everyone under-budgets. WordPress sites especially: plugins, security and updates are ongoing.
  • The domain & hosting — the cheapest part ($10–30/year + hosting).

Where the $5,000 goes

Pay a freelancer or agency $5,000 and most of it is time — discovery calls, draft rounds, revisions, emails. The output is genuinely good, but a meaningful chunk of that money is overhead and revision loops, not website. That's not a criticism; that's how professional services work.

The insight: if you can eliminate the revision loop — by getting the plan and copy right on the first pass — a great site can cost dramatically less. Which is exactly what bukkyai was built to do.

The real running costs nobody counts

Notice: a static site hosted anywhere has near-zero ongoing cost beyond domain + hosting. A platform-locked site charges you monthly forever just to keep your own content online.

So what should a small business actually pay?

  • Under $50/month total, one-time build — for most local businesses (restaurants, salons, trades, clinics) whose site needs to be clear, fast and findable. That's the bukkyai path: describe your business, approve the plan, own the files.
  • $1,000–3,000 one-time — if you need something genuinely custom and you have the patience to manage a freelancer.
  • $5,000+ — for brands where the website is a product: e-commerce at scale, custom apps, enterprise design.
The takeaway: most of what you pay for is planning and words, not "a website." Get those right once and a good site is cheap. Get them wrong and no budget fixes it.
