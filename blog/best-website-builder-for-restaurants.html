Best website builder for restaurants (2026)

By the bukkyai team · 6 min read

A restaurant website has one job: get a hungry person to pick up the phone, book a table, or walk through the door. Everything else — the parallax, the background video of the chef — is decoration that gets in the way.

Here's what a restaurant website actually needs, and the honest options for building one.

What a restaurant site must have

Menu — readable on a phone, and easy to update
Hours & location — including holiday hours, first thing on the page
Reservations — a button or link that works (not just a phone number at the bottom)
A real photo of your food — one good one beats a gallery of ten
Mobile-first — half your traffic is phones, and Google ranks mobile versions
Local SEO — the map, the "hours" that show in search, the link to directions

That's the whole checklist. Most builders can do all of it — the differences are speed, cost and how painful updates are.

The honest builder rankings

1. bukkyai — describe your restaurant and the whole site comes back: pages planned, copy written, design matched to your vibe. Menu section, hours, reservations button, a map — all built in. You edit in the preview and publish to your own domain. Built for exactly this checklist.

2. Squarespace / Wix — genuinely nice restaurant templates, especially for food photography. Solid, but you write everything yourself, pick from templates everyone else uses, and you're locked in with a monthly fee.

3. WordPress + theme — maximum control if you're technical or paying a developer. For a solo owner, it's the most maintenance for the least payoff.

4. Toast / OpenTable / Yelp page — not a website, but worth saying: for many casual spots, a clean reservation page plus a good Google Business Profile covers 80% of customers. A real site is what you add on top to look established.

The thing restaurants always forget: the menu

A PDF menu is a trap — it's slow on phones and Google can't read it (so "menu" searches miss you). Your menu should be real text on the page: Google indexes it, phones render it fast, and you can update prices without firing up a PDF editor.

Most owners put this off, which is why so many restaurant sites have a gray "Menu coming soon" box for months. Decide the format first — text on the page — and the rest is easy.

How long does it take?

Honest answer: with a traditional builder, a weekend to a month, most of it writing and formatting the menu. With bukkyai, you type one sentence about the restaurant, approve the plan, and edit — most owners go from zero to published in an afternoon.

The takeaway: a restaurant site needs menu, hours, reservations and a photo — readable on a phone. Pick the builder that gets you there fastest, and make the menu real text, not a PDF.
